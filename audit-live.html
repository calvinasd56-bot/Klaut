Top three things keeping this off the public web.

One — the Industries section shows 7 SHIPPED under Inventory Management and 4 SHIPPED under Marketplace. We have not shipped seven inventory engagements. We have not shipped four marketplace engagements. Any prospect who asks for one will catch us. Remove them today, or replace them with a number we can defend on a call.

Two — the four-service grid uses Consulting / Chief AI Officer / Klaut Governance / Audit. The founding strategy doc names the four components Foundation / Chief AI Officer / Klaut Governance / Flow. The footer uses a third variant. Pick one set of four names and apply it everywhere — the site, the pitch deck, every email signature.

Three — the hero subhead reads Consult, Construct, Deploy. The flow section walks through six phases: Consult / Diagnose / Design / Build / Deploy / Autonomy. A visitor scrolls one screen and the message has already changed shape. Match them.

Everything else on this audit is downstream of those three. The grammar fixes are visible-in-a-screenshot stuff. The AI-writing flags are about whether the page sounds like Klaut or sounds like every other AI vendor's homepage. The scalability findings are about what breaks when traffic actually arrives — no lead form, no analytics, no FAQ, no case study to show the people who click "Explore case studies."

Estimated work to close P0 + P1: two focused sessions. Estimated time to clear P2 and reach a confident public launch: two to three weeks.

Copy & Wording

A landing page is one sustained argument. Every section either reinforces the message or contradicts it. Right now the site contradicts itself within one scroll.

P0

"Consult, Construct, Deploy" doesn't match the actual flow.

Hero · subhead vs. #flow

The hero promises a three-step engagement. The Flow section walks through six phases: Consult / Diagnose / Design / Build / Deploy / Autonomy. A reader scrolls once and the story has changed.

Fix

Decide which is the canonical model. If three, kill the six-phase section. If six, rewrite the hero subhead. My preference: the six-phase model is closer to what we actually do — rewrite the hero to "Consult. Diagnose. Build. Operate." as four short clauses.

P0

Three different names for the four-service architecture.

#ecosystems vs. footer vs. strategy doc

The Ecosystems grid lists Consulting / Chief AI Officer / Klaut Governance / Audit. The footer lists Consult / Chief AI Officer / Governance / Audit. The internal strategy doc (klaud.md) defines the four components as Foundation / Chief AI Officer / Klaut Governance / Flow.

This is the most damaging single inconsistency on the site. A reader who looks at the grid, then the footer, then a one-pager we send them will see three different vocabularies for the same offering.

Lock the four names. My recommendation: align the site with the strategy doc — Foundation / Chief AI Officer / Klaut Governance / Flow — and use those exact words in the grid, the footer, every nav, and every email signature. If we keep "Audit" for the fourth product, then update klaud.md to match. Either direction, but pick one.

Scalability

What breaks when traffic actually arrives? Lead capture, qualification, content depth, SEO leverage. These don't block launch on day one — they cap growth from week two onward.

P1

Lead capture is mailto only.

All CTAs

Every conversion path on the site opens the user's default mail client. That works for the first five inbounds. It does not work for the first fifty. There is no structured intake, no qualification questions, no calendar handoff, no CRM hook.

Fix

Add a real lead form on the final CTA — three fields (name, company, what they need). Pipe it to a single inbox + a CRM (HubSpot free tier is fine for stage one). Keep one "Email instead" as a fallback for buyers who prefer it.

Verdict & Roadmap

One paragraph of verdict, three columns of work, one definition of done. Anything not in the P0 column does not block launch — but everything in P0 does.

Verdict. The English landing is not ready for public traffic. The visual design works. The structure works. The content is the gap — three contradictory naming conventions for the same four-service architecture, two fake shipping counts, four small grammar errors in copy that's one screenshot away from a competitor's Slack channel. None of these are hard fixes. All of them are necessary.

Definition of done for launch. Every P0 box on this audit is checked. Every P1 box is either checked or has a written reason for deferral logged in the project doc. The progress bar at the top of this page reads at least 20 of 35 resolved — which is the math of clearing all P0s plus all P1s — before we move marketing off "soft launch" and into anything paid.
